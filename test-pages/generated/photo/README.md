# Test pages: "photo" category (phone-photo / scan capture conditions)

Six single-page answer sheets for the **Biology - Genetics** paper (`backend/rubrics/biology_genetics_v1.json`, Q1-Q5).
Handwriting ranges from neat to average. Each sheet has a different capture problem, font, ink, paper and student ability.

- Drawn once by a seeded generator (removed; see `test-pages/README.md`).
- Handwriting: each sheet uses its own font. On top of the font, characters get random baseline, size, spacing and alpha (ink-skip) variation, each word gets its own rotation, the baseline drifts like a random walk, and each line gets slant (shear) and a small rotation.
- Live test: `LIVE_TEST_NAME=photo PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe test-pages/run_live_test.py test-pages/generated/photo`

Live run: 2026-11-20, model `claude-sonnet-5-5`, OCR threshold 0.80.

## Observations

- None of the six capture conditions sent a page to review. All six COMPLETED with confidence 0.85-0.94, and every final score came within ±2 of the planned score. The upside-down scan (06) and the q25 compressed photo (04) gave the lowest confidences, but both stayed above 0.80. 06 was also the weakest student, with one blank question and one struck-out answer, so its low score comes from content, not from the orientation.
- EXIF orientation works (05). The backend has no rotation correction for pixel-rotated pages without EXIF (06). It sent the page upside down and the model read it anyway.
- The pipeline shows no low-resolution warning for the 720x960 photo, because the warning only fires when the long edge is under 800 px. The normaliser also re-encodes small or heavily compressed JPEGs as PNG: 150 KB became 1.0 MB for 01, and 88 KB became 783 KB for 04. This is harmless but inflates the upload.
- The grader sticks closely to the rubric wording. "Flat face" was not credited as a Down's symptom (01 Q5) because it is not in the expected list. Answers that named a test cross without its purpose lost 0.5 (01 Q1). "Back cross" and "monohybrid cross" got no credit for the naming mark.
